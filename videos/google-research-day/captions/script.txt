I just spent the day inside Google Research in Silicon Valley.
And honestly, some of the most interesting AI announcements had nothing to do with chatbots.
Google showed AI that can analyze the entire planet, help scientists discover new things, check research papers, and even power tiny wearable devices.
Let me show you what actually matters.
First — Google Earth AI.
Imagine asking AI:
Where should I build my next data center?
Which neighborhoods are most at risk of flooding?
Or where should a city invest in new infrastructure?
Google's new Geospatial Reasoning agent connects Gemini with satellite imagery, weather models, population data, and more than 70 geospatial tools.
And here's the interesting part.
Instead of just generating an answer, the agent plans a workflow, gathers the relevant data, and performs spatial analysis.
Google is also opening experimental access through Google Earth and an agent-to-agent API.
For developers, this could become a completely new category of AI applications.
Second — AI scientists.
Google demonstrated a multi-agent system that helps scientists reproduce previous research and explore new discoveries.
One example?
Using AI-assisted research to design urinary catheters that performed better at preventing bacterial infections in simulations.
And Google's AI Co-Scientist is exploring new scientific hypotheses, including potential drug treatments.
This is where agents get interesting.
Not just writing code — helping researchers discover things.
Third — Deep Review.
Everyone is using AI to generate content.
But who's checking whether that content is actually correct?
Google is making Deep Review publicly available.
It's an AI system built to scrutinize complex technical documents, check reasoning, and identify possible mistakes.
An earlier version was used with more than 16,000 research paper submissions.
And now the technology is expanding beyond scientific papers to other complex documents.
Fourth — Edge AI hardware.
Google previewed a next-generation, low-power AI chip developed with Synaptics.
Think smart glasses, smartwatches, cameras, and voice-enabled devices.
Instead of sending everything to the cloud, more AI processing could happen directly on your device.
For hardware developers, this is a space worth watching.
Fifth — AI for numbers.
Google has TimesFM and TabFM.
These are foundation models designed for time-series and tabular data.
Instead of training a separate forecasting model for every business problem, developers can use pretrained models to make predictions.
Google is planning to integrate these capabilities into Gemini for business.
Sixth — Medical AI.
Google's AMIE is a research system for medical conversations and clinical reasoning.
In a study involving 98 patients before urgent care visits, clinicians reported that AMIE's summaries helped them prepare in 75% of cases.
And MedGemma provides open medical AI models that developers can adapt for specialized healthcare applications.
These systems are still subject to important clinical and safety limitations.
But the research is moving beyond basic symptom-checking chatbots.
Seventh — AI predicting disasters.
Google's Groundsource uses Gemini to turn historical flood reports into structured data.
The resulting dataset contains more than 2.6 million flood events.
That helped researchers build a model that can predict the likelihood of urban flash flooding up to 24 hours in advance.
Now that's a real-world application of AI.
And Google also showed AI-generated educational simulations and a collaborative prototyping tool called Play with Putty.
But here's my biggest takeaway from today's event.
We're moving from AI that generates text...
To AI that can reason over physical-world data, check complex scientific work, support medical research, and run on specialized devices.
And for software engineers, the opportunity isn't just to build another chatbot.
It's to combine AI models with tools, real-world data, and domain-specific expertise.
That's where I think the next wave of valuable AI products will come from.
I also met the researchers building some of these systems, so more technical deep dives are coming.
Which of these announcements would you actually build with?
